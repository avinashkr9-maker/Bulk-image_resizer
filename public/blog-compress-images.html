How to Compress Images Without Losing Quality - Complete Guide 2025

6 minute read · June 2025

Images are typically the largest files on any website, often accounting for 60 to 70 percent of a page's total file size. When images are not properly compressed, they slow down page load times, increase bandwidth costs, hurt search engine rankings and frustrate mobile users on slower connections. The good news is that modern image compression technology can reduce image file sizes by 50 to 80 percent with no visible difference in quality.

This guide explains how image compression works, which settings to use for different situations, and how to compress hundreds of images at once without spending hours doing it manually.

What is Image Compression?

Image compression is the process of reducing the file size of an image file by encoding the image data more efficiently. There are two types of compression: lossy and lossless.

Lossy compression permanently removes some image data that the human eye cannot easily detect. JPEG uses lossy compression. When you save a JPEG at 90% quality, you are telling the encoder to remove approximately 10% of the image data that contributes least to visual quality. The result is a file that is much smaller but looks virtually identical to the original to most viewers.

Lossless compression reduces file size without removing any image data. It works by finding more efficient ways to encode the same information. PNG uses lossless compression, which is why PNG files are generally larger than JPEGs. WebP supports both lossy and lossless compression, giving you the best of both worlds.

Choosing the Right Quality Setting

The quality setting in image compression tools controls how aggressively the encoder removes image data. Higher quality means larger files but better visual quality. Lower quality means smaller files but potentially visible compression artefacts like blockiness or colour banding.

Recommended Quality Settings by Use Case
  • Hero images and photography portfolios: 90 to 95% quality
  • Product images for ecommerce: 85 to 90% quality
  • Blog post images and editorial photos: 80 to 85% quality
  • Thumbnails and preview images: 70 to 80% quality
  • Background images: 65 to 75% quality

For most ecommerce and website use cases, 80 to 85% quality is the sweet spot. At this setting, images look excellent to customers while file sizes are dramatically reduced compared to the original uncompressed files.

WebP vs JPEG vs PNG - Which Format Compresses Best?

WebP
25 to 35% smaller than JPEG at the same quality. Best overall compression. Supports transparency. Supported by all modern browsers. Best choice for websites in 2025.
JPEG
Universal compatibility. Works on all devices and platforms including older systems. No transparency support. Good compression but not as efficient as WebP. Best for email and print.
PNG
Lossless compression only. Largest file sizes. Essential for logos and images requiring transparency. Not recommended for photographs due to large file size.

How Much Can You Reduce File Size?

The amount of file size reduction you can achieve depends on the original image, the compression format and quality setting you choose, and the content of the image itself. Images with large areas of solid colour or simple patterns compress much better than complex photographs with lots of detail and colour variation.

As a practical example, a typical product photograph saved directly from a camera or smartphone might be 8 to 12 megabytes as an uncompressed TIFF or high quality JPEG. After compressing to WebP at 85% quality, the same image typically becomes 200 to 400 kilobytes, a reduction of 95 to 97 percent. This means you can store 25 times more images in the same amount of storage space, and your website pages will load 5 to 10 times faster for customers on mobile connections.

Impact of Image Compression on SEO

Google uses page loading speed as a ranking factor in its search algorithm. Slow pages rank lower in search results than fast pages, all else being equal. Since images are typically the largest files on a web page, compressing images is one of the most effective ways to improve page speed and therefore SEO rankings.

Google measures page speed using Core Web Vitals, a set of metrics that include Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), which measures how quickly the largest visible element on a page loads. For most pages, the largest element is a hero image or product photo. Compressing this image can dramatically improve your LCP score.

According to Google research, pages that load in 1 to 3 seconds have a 32% higher probability of a bounce compared to pages that load in under 1 second. Pages that take 5 seconds to load have a 90% higher bounce rate. Compressed images are one of the most direct paths to faster loading pages and lower bounce rates.

How to Compress Images in Bulk

If you have a website with hundreds or thousands of images, compressing them one at a time is not practical. Bulk image compression tools allow you to upload an entire folder of images and compress all of them simultaneously with the same quality settings.

The process is straightforward. You select your quality level (80 to 85% is recommended for most cases), choose whether to keep the original format or convert everything to WebP, upload your folder of images, and download all compressed images as a ZIP file. For a folder of 200 product images, this process typically takes 2 to 3 minutes compared to hours of manual editing.

Best Practices for Image Compression

  • Always keep your original files: Compression is not reversible. Keep your original high resolution uncompressed images as backups before compressing.
  • Compress before uploading: Always compress images before uploading to your website or ecommerce platform. Do not rely on the platform to compress images for you as they may not do it optimally.
  • Use WebP for web: Convert product images and blog photos to WebP format for the best combination of quality and file size for web use.
  • Test on mobile: Always check how compressed images look on a mobile device. Mobile screens can sometimes reveal compression artefacts that are less visible on desktop monitors.
  • Batch process your entire library: Set aside time to compress your entire existing image library at once rather than doing it image by image as you upload new content.

Summary

Image compression is one of the most impactful optimisations you can make for your website performance, SEO rankings and user experience. By compressing images to 80 to 85% quality and converting them to WebP format, you can typically reduce image file sizes by 75 to 80% with no visible quality difference. This results in faster page load times, better Core Web Vitals scores, higher search rankings and lower bounce rates from impatient mobile users.
